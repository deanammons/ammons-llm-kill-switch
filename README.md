# The Last Kill Switch: An Exploration of Mortality and Gods

**Dean Ammons**

*Information, Intervention, and the Future of Human and Synthetic Life*

**Information Flow Series · Paper IV · November 15, 2026**

**How can dangerous authority be ended while preserving life and a recoverable future?**

[Read the paper (PDF)](The_Last_Kill_Switch.pdf) · [Original publication on Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/24254090) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24254090) · [Press kit](PRESS_KIT.md)

## The question

The last human presses the kill switch. Nothing happens.

The opening thought experiment asks what must remain enforceable when an intelligence can copy itself, delegate work, retain state, or become essential to the people trying to stop it. A second question follows: could a finite, uncertain lifetime limit the persistence of power?

## Overview

This paper connects influence, tracing, provenance, intervention, preservation, and recovery within one mathematical lifecycle. It examines conditions under which dangerous authority can be restricted while protecting essential services and limiting the power of those who intervene.

Its mortality hypothesis asks whether finite existence can constrain persistent authority or change incentives. The analysis treats lifetime, copying, survival uncertainty, cooperation, and the governance of intervention as separate questions.

## What the paper develops

- **Intervention across the complete continuation path.** The analysis includes retained state, credentials, copied systems, delegated actions, queued work, and reconstruction.
- **Containment, preservation, and recovery under one policy.** A joint reachability construction requires the same policy to restrict danger, preserve the adopted life and rights protections, and leave a feasible route to essential services.
- **A formal mortality hypothesis.** Survival laws and lineage conditions distinguish eventual ending, enforceable deadlines, hidden expiration, and the continuation of surviving copies.
- **Incentives and reciprocal protection.** Exact constructions show that increased mortality can improve an incentive in one setting and reduce cooperation in another, making the governing assumptions consequential.

## Research scope

This is a mathematical research preprint and synthesis. It presents conditional results, stipulated constructions, and open research propositions. The mortality hypothesis remains a question for further investigation; the paper reports no new shutdown implementation, model experiment, or empirical test of synthetic mortality.
